These clips come from a cut-cell finite element method for phase-field fracture that I am developing at UC Berkeley. Holes, notches, and the cracks themselves are cut through one fixed Cartesian grid, so complex geometries and evolving crack paths need no remeshing, and explicit dynamics runs on a GPU. In quasi-static benchmarks the method matches body-fitted finite elements with about a third of the degrees of freedom.

Supershear cracks in stretched soft sheets

Problem setup after Tian & Gong, J. Mech. Phys. Solids 202, 106191 (2025). Cracks faster than the shear-wave speed, with oscillating paths, were observed in stretched hydrogels by Wang, Shi & Fineberg, Science 381, 415 (2023).

Brittle plates with pores

Plates and pore layouts from the PMMA experiments of Carlsson & Isaksson, Eng. Fract. Mech. 206, 526–540 (2019), as restated by Peng, Chen & Bobaru, Int. J. Fract. (2023). Each movie shows the plate colored by strain-energy density, the crack path in the reference grid, and the cut cells at the leading tip. Cases A–C use the same load.

Hydraulic fracturing

The fluid pressure acts directly on the cut crack faces. Each movie shows the sheet (displacement magnified; blue: fluid), the pressures against the injected volume, the cut cells at a crack tip, and the distance of the crack tips from the borehole.
